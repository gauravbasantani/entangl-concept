Concept · step-by-step execution on iPad

The step was written against an older drawing

A technician is switching a UPS to maintenance bypass, following an AI-written procedure on an iPad. At step 4, where this opens, the site record shows the one-line drawing changed after the procedure was approved: the breaker was relabelled. This sketch shows how the screen stops them, shows exactly what changed, and lets them carry on safely or call the engineer. Tap through it.

WHERE THIS COMES FROM

The quiet mistakes are the dangerous ones

In Gauravide, a receipt app I designed and directed Claude Code to build, a £20 receipt once reached Splitwise as $20 with no error shown. Nothing looked wrong. A step that names a breaker from an old drawing looks just as normal. So the screen checks the source before the step, not after.

1 · STOP ONLY WHEN IT MATTERS

Most steps are one instruction and one big button. The hold point is the only loud thing on the screen, so it can't be confused with a normal step or tapped past.

2 · SHOW THE CHANGE, NOT A WARNING

Rev B next to rev C, the old label struck through. The technician can match it to what's printed on the panel in front of them, instead of reading a paragraph.

3 · EVERY STEP LEAVES EVIDENCE

Readings, the label they saw and who they called go into the record with a time. The reviewing engineer and the auditor read the same list later.

4 · SIX LANGUAGES

Switch to ES. Equipment names stay exactly as printed on the equipment, in every language. Only the words around them change.

A concept by Gaurav Basantani for the Design Engineer role at Entangl. Not made with Entangl, and it uses no Entangl data, brand or screens. The site, equipment, procedure and people are made up, and the steps are simplified for illustration; they are not a real switching procedure.
